## Breakpoints and the compact layout

The library sorts a chart into one of three layouts by container width: full above 700px, medium from 400 to 700px, and compact below 400px. Compact drops value labels, uses the fewest [axis ticks](/docs/the-spec/axes/) and keeps at most one callout inline. Any other callout becomes a numbered marker, with its text in a note under the plot.

In the two batting charts above, the year ticks go from four (1960, 1980, 2000, 2020) to three (1960, 2000, 2020), and the 2020 callout turns into marker 1 with its note under the plot. Notes are numbered by their place in the spec's `annotations` list, which is why the 2020 callout is listed first.

Height counts too. A chart 350px tall or shorter drops the subtitle and keeps its title, and one under 100px tall drops the title as well.

The breakpoints follow the width of the container, not the screen. A chart in a 300px sidebar on a desktop monitor gets the compact layout, so test one width inside each band (360, 560 and your full column) instead of one phone.

## Setting up a chart for phones

Write a second, shorter [title and subtitle](/docs/framing/chrome/) for compact width and put it in `overrides`. The title of the last chart here takes two lines at 720px and four at 360px, which pushes the plot down, and the layout won't shorten a headline for you.

Pick the callout that carries the point and give it `priority: 1`. Compact keeps the callout with the lowest `priority` number inline and numbers the rest, falling back to spec order when no callout has a priority. Callouts you write for phones in `overrides` are not held to the one-callout limit, so keep that list short.

Check that every line still has its name at its end. Three series fit at 360px. With more, `responsive: { labelSummary: true }` moves names and last values into a keyed list under the plot when they cannot fit, and a top legend, as the [direct labels post](/blog/direct-labels-vs-legends/) suggests for phones, also gives the lines the full width.

Set the height for the narrow shape. Height comes from the container, not the spec, and 360px wide by 440px tall is a different shape from 720 by 440. A time series usually reads better wider than tall, so check how steep the lines look before you keep the same height.

## The spec

`overrides.compact` is a patch laid over the spec when the container is under 400px. Here it changes the title and subtitle in `chrome`, which merge field by field into the base, swaps `annotations` for a single short callout, and sets the x axis `values` so the phone shows 1995, 2005, 2015 and 2024. It can also change labels and the legend. `medium` and `full` work the same way.

`responsive: { preserveEnd: true }` keeps the last tick on the time axis when ticks are thinned, so the final year stays on the axis at medium width. `endpointLabels` names each line at its right end, which is why the chart needs no legend.

`theme.colors.categorical` gives each series a light and a dark color, both readable as end-label text on their page. `chromeLayout: "subtract"` holds the figure at its fixed height, so a long title or a note for a demoted callout takes room from the plot instead of making the figure taller.

Every breakpoint and `responsive` option is listed in [the responsive docs](/docs/behavior/responsive/).
