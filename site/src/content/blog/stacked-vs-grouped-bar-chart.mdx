US solar generators that came online in 2024 add up to 31.2 gigawatts, 13 times the new natural gas capacity. Whether a chart makes that comparison easy depends on whether it puts each year's fuels end to end in a stack or side by side in a group. A stack shows the total and how the bottom segment moves. A group shows how the parts compare with each other.

## Stacked bars

The chart below stacks four fuels for each year, in gigawatts (GW) of nameplate capacity, the maximum output a generator is rated for. The column height is the four fuels' total, which reached 49.8 GW in 2024. Solar sits at the bottom, so its growth from 5.0 GW in 2018 to 31.2 GW in 2024 is easy to follow.

<Demo id="blog/capacity-stacked" />

The data counts only generators that still operate in one snapshot of the EIA's inventory. A plant that opened in 2018 and has since retired is missing, so older years are understated.

Now the comparisons are direct: in 2024 batteries (11.1 GW) added more than twice as much as wind (5.2 GW). The total is gone, though, and a reader who wants it has to add four bars. Four fuels across seven years is 28 bars, about as many as a grouped chart can hold before the bars get too thin to read.

## Order of the segments

Put the series the reader cares about at the bottom, where it starts at zero, and keep its order and color the same in every column. Past four or five segments the middle ones get hard to read, and a grouped chart or one panel per series works better.

Choose that order on purpose. Data often arrives sorted by name, and a stack drawn in that order puts whichever series sorts first at the bottom, whether or not the reader cares about it.

## Totals over time

Stacking is the right choice when the total is the story. Counted homelessness in the 50 states and DC fell to 544,084 people in 2016 and then rose 41% to 767,856 in 2024, and the stack shows that total while still splitting it into sheltered and unsheltered people. Unsheltered people, the top segment, rose from 172,035 to 271,350 over the same years, and that change is harder to see than the total.

If shares matter more than totals, normalize each column with a [100% stacked bar](/docs/part-to-whole/stacked-100/). For many periods, a [stacked area](/docs/change-over-time/stacked-area/) draws the same stack as a continuous shape.

## The spec

Both capacity charts use `mark: "bar"` with the series in `color`. The only difference is the value channel: `stack: "zero"` on `y` stacks the series from a zero baseline and `stack: null` places them side by side. Segments stack in the order of the data rows, and the rows list solar first, so solar is the bottom segment. The `domain` and `range` on the `color` scale pin each fuel to a color.

`labelAngle: 0` keeps the year labels level, and `values` on the x axis picks which years get a label, with an `overrides.compact` block labeling fewer of them on a phone. The homelessness chart adds one `text` annotation on the 2021 column, with `x: "2021"` placing it on that category.

The [grouped and stacked bars page](/docs/comparison/grouped-stacked/) shows how to print a value inside each segment.
