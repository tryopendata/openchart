Which states cut teacher pay the most? Between 1999-00 and 2021-22 the average public-school teacher salary, adjusted for inflation, fell in 36 of 51 jurisdictions (the 50 states and DC), according to the [National Center for Education Statistics](https://nces.ed.gov/programs/digest/d22/tables/dt22_211.60.asp). A histogram of those changes shows how many states fell by how much, but it cannot name a single one of them.

<Demo id="blog/teacher-pay-histogram" />

The tallest bar is the group of states whose pay fell by about 10%. Which states those are, the chart cannot say, and an unusual state is just a short bar at the edge.

## How a beeswarm plot works

A beeswarm plot draws each observation as its own dot along one axis, and nudges dots apart, off the axis, when they would overlap. The clusters that form are the shape of the distribution, and every state is still there to point at.

Read the swarm the way you would read a histogram: where dots crowd together, many states share a value. Of the 36 states that fell, 23 lost between 5% and 12%, and the 14 states and DC that gained are spread thinly along the right.

Only the position along the axis means anything. The nudge off the axis exists to keep dots from covering each other, so a dot sitting higher than its neighbor is not larger or later. Add a caption saying so for readers new to the chart.

## Splitting a swarm into groups

A second field turns one swarm into one lane per group. Here, each dot is one of the 125 college majors with at least 5,000 recent graduates, placed by its graduates' median pay, and each lane is a field of study.

<Demo id="blog/majors-pay-beeswarm" />

Engineering, in teal, has the widest spread, with majors across a $25,000 range. Many majors share the same pay, because the source rounds it, and identical values stack into columns of dots instead of covering each other.

Seventeen majors share a median of exactly $35,000, spread across 13 fields, and the column of dots at $35,000 shows each of them. A box plot draws the middle half of the values as one box, and it can't show that 17 majors share exactly $35,000.

A beeswarm shows one number at a time. To ask whether two numbers move together, such as pay and the share of women in a major, use a [scatter plot](/docs/correlation/scatter/) instead.

## How many points to plot

Every row gets a dot, so the plot runs out of room as the rows multiply. With thousands of points the dots either shrink until they are specks or stack so far off the axis that neighboring lanes overlap. A few hundred points is a practical ceiling. Past that, use a [histogram](/docs/distribution/histogram/) or a density curve and give up the individual dots.

At the other end, a handful of points that never overlap do not need dodging. A [tick strip](/docs/distribution/tick-strip/) draws one tick per value on a shared axis and is simpler.

## The spec

Set `mark` to `"beeswarm"` and put the value field on `encoding.x`. The teacher chart also puts a field with one value, `lane`, on `y` with `axis: false`, so all the dots share one lane and the two text annotations have a y position to label. Its `color` field is `direction`, which each row carries as `Fell` or `Rose`, worked out from the change before charting. `theme.colors.categorical` gives the two colors in domain order, each a `{ light, dark }` pair so the dots stay clear on a dark page.

`detail: { field: "state" }` puts the state's name at the top of each tooltip, and `tooltip` lists the rows under it, with `format: "+.1f"` printing the change to one decimal. `legend: { show: false }` drops the legend because the subtitle names the colors. On a phone a chart moves annotations into tooltips, and `responsive.compactAnnotationLimit: 2` keeps both labels on the chart.

For lanes, add a nominal field on `encoding.y`, as the majors chart does with its field of study. Its `sort` object, `{ field: "pay", op: "median", order: "ascending" }`, orders the lanes by median pay, highest at the top because `y` lists categories from the bottom up. Swap `x` and `y` to turn the swarm vertical.

To size each dot by a second number, such as the number of graduates, see the [beeswarm page](/docs/distribution/beeswarm/).
