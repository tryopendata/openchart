In the latest readings, the 30-year mortgage rate is 0.87 points above a year earlier, a dozen eggs cost $1.17 less than about a year before, and the share of US job postings that mention remote or hybrid work is up 0.40 points. A KPI dashboard should make answers like those readable in a few seconds. Each number gets a tile with three parts: the latest reading, a comparison and a small line for the shape.

<Demo id="blog/kpi-dashboard" />

## What goes in a KPI tile

A number alone does not say whether it is high, so every tile needs a comparison. Pick the one the reader acts on: the previous period for numbers that move week to week, the same period a year earlier for anything with a season, or a target when there is one. These tiles use a year earlier, which also cancels any seasonal swing.

## Sparklines

A sparkline is a line chart stripped of its axes, gridlines, legend and source line, small enough to sit beside a number. In a box this small, axis labels would take more room than the line, and the reader wants direction, not exact values.

<Demo id="blog/kpi-sparkline-bare" />

The line carries no scale, so it cannot say that the climb was from 2.65% to above 7%. The number has to sit beside it, here as its title.

## Labeling a tile without axes

Dropping the axes and chrome removes the unit, the period and the source, so the tile has to supply them another way. The unit and the period go in the title, as in "weekly" and the % sign on the number. The source goes under the tile, as the line under each chart here does.

Name what was measured. The remote-work tile counts the share of Indeed job postings that mention remote or hybrid work, not the share of people who work remotely, and a title that said "remote work" would claim far more than the data shows.

Each tile scales its line to its own range, so two lines of the same height can stand for very different changes. Say so in the subtitle, as the dashboard does, or share a y domain when the sizes should compare.

Show gaps as gaps. The egg source has no price for October 2025, so the egg line breaks there instead of drawing a straight segment across the missing month. For the same reason the egg tile compares October 2026 with September 2025, the closest earlier month, and its label names that month.

## Laying out the grid

The dashboard is one chart split into panels, one per KPI, each with its own y scale. A panel needs at least 200 pixels, so three fit side by side on a desktop and they stack into one column on a phone.

The metrics row shows only when the chart is at least 480 pixels wide and leaves at least 150 pixels for the plot. On a phone the metrics row drops out and the compact subtitle carries each latest reading and its change.

Keep the tiles in a row the same height, and put first the one the reader acts on first. Equal heights make the lines easier to compare for direction.

## The spec

The dashboard is a line chart with `encoding.facet` on the tile name and `columns: 3`, and `resolve: { scale: { y: "independent" } }` gives each panel its own range. `axis: false` on `x` and `y` removes both axes, and `scale: { zero: false }` lets each line use the full panel height. A row with a `null` value breaks the line, which is how the missing egg month shows.

`metrics` is an array of cells shown above the plot, each a `label` and a `value`, with an optional `delta` and a `deltaTone` of `"up"` or `"down"` for its color. `overrides.compact.chrome` swaps in a phone title and subtitle. The code that builds the spec writes every label, value and delta from the same rows that draw the lines.

The sparkline sets `display: "sparkline"`, which strips the axes, gridlines, legend and source. A `chrome.title` set explicitly stays, which is how the number sits above the line. A sparkline colors itself by trend unless `mark.stroke` is set, and a y `scale.domain` with room above the peak keeps the line clear of the title. In the single tile, `mark.point: "last"` puts a dot on the latest reading.
